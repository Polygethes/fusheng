# Interview Protocol And Stage Gates

Use the same stages for every character. Skip questions already answered by authoritative material, but never skip the gate. Ask one to three questions per turn.

## Stage 0: Scope and authority

Collect scene id, character id, source materials, current runtime authority files, and the intended point in story time. Decide which existing file owns scene facts, psychology, voice, relationships, memory, presence, and candidate compilation.

**Gate:** no duplicate live authority is planned.

## Stage 1: Confirm objective scene facts

Complete the shared scene contract: ownership, operators, zones, routes, concrete positions, privacy, resources, object attachment states, roles, affordances, current services and prices, separate transaction phases, impossibilities, atmosphere, temptations, hazards, information routes, and entry/exit feasibility.

Separate hard impossibility from social pressure. “No object, no route, no physical capacity” may remove a candidate. “Rude, forbidden, shameful, reputation-damaging, likely to be stopped, or dangerous to bystanders” stays attemptable when physically possible and must enter the Character Agent's appraisal. An operator's objection becomes a blocking force only when that operator actually perceives, chooses, and performs an intervention or when a real barrier is already in place.

**Gate:** the user confirms the material facts; no character preference, preferred position, or expected action appears as a world rule.

For a service venue, verify that the visitor can request, defer, decline, remain without buying when physically possible, or leave. Keep visitor request, operator response, fulfillment, consumption, and settlement distinct. The author confirms available facts and customs, not what the visitor will order.

## Stage 2: Confirm the character fact card

Separate:

- identity and public role;
- why the person is here now;
- body, possessions, duties, debts, promises, and current constraints;
- trained perception, professional competence, procedural habits, equipment discipline, and conditions that degrade current performance;
- what they know by name, recognize directly, infer, or heard second-hand;
- confirmed facts, inferences, unknowns, conflicts, and story-open choices.

**Gate:** the user confirms basic facts before numerical calibration.

## Stage 3: Anchor desire and threat

Ask behaviorally:

1. What outcome does this person want for themself right now?
2. What would count as concrete progress and verified satisfaction?
3. What self-state can they least tolerate discovering or becoming?
4. What happens when the desire is blocked rather than resolved?

Reject desires written as duties, prohibitions, bookkeeping rules, judgments about another person, or negated sentences.

**Gate:** at least one positive self-owned desire, one threat, one progress path, and one satisfaction condition exist.

## Stage 4: Calibrate controlled contrasts

Change one variable at a time and ask what the person notices, privately appraises, does, says, suppresses, or postpones.

| Contrast | What it identifies |
|---|---|
| public / private | approval sensitivity, exposure cost, norm cost |
| stranger / intimate / rival / authority | relationship modulation |
| direct evidence / trusted report / rumor | epistemic weights |
| rested / exhausted / injured | state sensitivity |
| resource present / absent | environmental opportunity and temptation relevance |
| low / high collateral trouble | inhibition independent from desire |
| first refusal / repeated frustration | desire retention and escalation |
| safe / irreversible consequence | major-choice threshold |

Record every answer with an evidence id and scope. Do not universalize domain-specific control.

**Gate:** at least two behavioral domains and three controlled contrasts have evidence.

Do not infer a fixed action from professional competence. Test the same spatial or equipment problem under different goals and body states: competence should improve noticing, option quality, and execution while allowing different choices.

When a scene offers food, drink, lodging, trade, or aid, vary body need, price, owned resources, expected stay, information value, social exposure, and delay one at a time. Infer sensitivities rather than a fixed buying habit; the Character Agent owns the resulting choice.

## Stage 5: Anchor speech as action

Collect accepted speech or silence for at least:

- pressure or challenge;
- genuine evaluation or praise;
- request, refusal, or boundary;
- future commitment;
- a situation where silence is more useful than speaking.

Infer sentence shape, directness, explanation cost, preferred pragmatic forms, pressure mutation, relationship registers, and forbidden generic language. Preserve exact lines only in `calibration-evidence.json`; compile aggregate tendencies into the foundation and never pass the quotations to the Agent as catchphrases, lexicon, or regexes.

**Gate:** at least three distinct speech functions plus a silence rule.

## Stage 6: Relationships, memory, and major choices

Define relationship changes by dimension. Distinguish familiarity, trust, affection, care, dependence, competence respect, rivalry, resentment, fear, and obligation.

Create initial memory only from owned experience, self-action, inference, or sourced report. Preserve semantic roles and source confidence. Record open commitments directionally.

Keep identity conflict story-open. A major choice may revise self-narrative only when it is consequential, freely chosen among meaningful alternatives, and backed by a remembered event.

**Gate:** relationship rules do not collapse dimensions; memory contains no omniscient fact; open choices remain unresolved.

## Stage 7: Holdouts and runtime mapping

Generate at least three situations unseen by the calibrated character model. Include one counterexample to the most tempting stereotype. Let the generic cognitive core form current candidates from perceived world affordances, then let the Character Agent choose action, speech/silence, private motive, relevant memory, and relationship deltas from the accepted foundation. Present the derived result to the author for authenticity acceptance or correction; do not ask the author to supply the scene answer first.

On correction, repair the smallest reusable source: foundation fact, qualitative weight/range, knowledge provenance, relationship interpretation, competence or procedural habit, neutral spatial affordance, object attachment transition, or consequence expectation. Preserve close-call choices as genuine Agent-owned alternatives. Never compile an author correction directly into a one-off response lookup.

Map the accepted packet to exact runtime authority surfaces. Do not edit runtime before the anchor is at least `calibrated`; do not mark it `validated` before holdouts pass.

**Gate:** three holdouts accepted and every runtime surface named.

## Stage 8: Behavioral acceptance

Run deterministic counterexamples and, separately, a real-model scene when available. Verify:

- the Agent may choose a feasible non-top mathematical candidate;
- a blocked desire remains unresolved;
- a rumor affects search/uncertainty without becoming fact;
- distance and barriers prevent knowledge;
- scene resources create character-specific opportunity;
- dialogue reflects the voice contract without repeating anchors;
- a transaction does not force departure;
- a consequential choice changes self-narrative only after it occurs.

Keep deterministic contract success separate from literary/naturalness acceptance.
